1. The direction so far

Confirmed server identity: A fantasy/tech RPG SMP server. SMP means survival multiplayer: players share a persistent survival world. Fantasy, technology and RPG-style character development are part of the server’s intended identity, alongside building, industry, magic and group expeditions. Players should be able to choose their own main interest without being forced through every other system.

Confirmed starting experience: There will be a starter town with player onboarding. The town is the planned introduction to the server, not an optional future content idea. Its layout, exact onboarding flow and implementation are still to be designed.

The server identity and starter-town requirement were confirmed by the owner for this revision. They are design commitments, not claims that the town is built or that all systems have passed multiplayer testing. The owner has also confirmed a server-wide currency, unified Tensura/Solo Leveling stats, classes and races, and levels plus ranks for all mobs. These are integration requirements, not proof that current mods already provide them. A custom quest engine or linear campaign is not implied.

The project already has broad content coverage. The main planning job now is to turn that content into a clear, enjoyable experience: why someone joins, what they do first, how they find a direction, how friends play together and why they return. More mods are not the default answer. [S1–S4]

Established direction

  • Build a fantasy/tech RPG SMP server with a starter town that provides player onboarding. These are confirmed product requirements.
  • Offer two adventure routes: optional hub-accessed, instanced procedurally generated dungeons, and normal exploration of mod-added dungeons in the shared world. This is a confirmed experience direction, not an implemented instancing system.
  • Technology and fantasy belong in the same sandbox. Modern Industrialization and AE2 provide the industrial/storage direction, alongside several different magic and character-progression systems.
  • Use one server-wide currency, unify Tensura/Solo Leveling stats, classes and races, and give all mobs levels and ranks. Implementation and balance remain to be designed.
  • Support anvil/special-UI equipment customisation costing the server’s universal in-game currency, not real-world money: adopt another item’s appearance or special ability without copying its stats. Include separate enhancement, enchantment and socketing systems.
  • Use one unified real-player grouping system integrated with FTB Teams and FTB Chunks for membership, chunk claims and player rules. If Tensura or Solo Leveling supplies real-player guild/group features, integrate them into this same system rather than maintain competing memberships. Native guild support is not yet verified.
  • Replace equipment that would drop from killed mobs with redeemable equipment mob tokens. At redemption, player keep-filters retain matching equipment and convert rejected results directly into universal currency without creating the rejected equipment item. This is a confirmed requirement; performance benefits remain unmeasured.
  • Make Waystones and collectible mounts the main transport systems. Ground, water and flying mounts must be summonable, invincible and support more than one player riding together. Disable independent player flight. Exact acquisition, seats, travel rules and implementation remain open.
  • Allow player-owned pets: idle pets are invincible and return to their recorded idle location after a delay if moved; each player may bring only one following pet. Following pets ignore environmental damage but can take mob damage, and respawn after a timer if killed. Timer values and implementation remain open.
  • Allow adventure parties of at most four total members, counting real players and NPC companions. Leveling XP per member is 100% solo, 55% with two members, 40% with three and 40% with four, relative to the solo base reward. NPC companions use the pet protection/respawn logic but occupy party slots rather than the one-pet allowance.
  • Create a custom Tensura skill catalogue and mob-to-skill mappings covering all mobs not already supported by Tensura-compatible mods/integrations. Preserve verified existing mappings; exact skills, acquisition/usage rules and implementation remain to design.
  • Offer optional purchasable building plots in the starter town/hub, paid for in universal in-game currency. Players may build their own house or other structure there, while retaining independent open-world chunk claiming and sandbox building.
  • Use distance from the starter town/hub as the current open-world danger direction: farther away means higher mob levels/ranks. Party loot distribution follows party settings, separately from the fixed party-XP schedule. Formulas, loot modes and defaults remain open.
  • Keep inventory on player death and deduct XP only from progress toward the next RPG level, without removing earned levels or unlocked abilities. Normal deaths respawn at the bed; instance deaths respawn at the portal entrance. Only a full party wipe resets a shared instance; solo death is a wipe for a solo run. Major dungeon rewards are granted at completion. Exact penalty and recovery/re-entry details remain open.
  • Provide complete magic-only, exploration-only and tech-only routes to shared server goals. Do not force players to personally progress another discipline; mixing playstyles is optional. Native discipline-specific mechanics may retain their own requirements, but must not become hidden gates on shared progression.
  • Host the shared world on Space-Node Minecraft hosting. The owner’s likely starting choice is the 12 GB plan; exact tier, region and purchase remain unconfirmed. This supersedes the earlier no-rental/e4mc direction. Performance and everyday usability remain priorities. Prism Launcher is the client-pack delivery route.
  • Create and its addons are excluded. Replacing their mechanical gameplay is not an outstanding requirement.
  • Keep useful existing content before adding speculative systems. Judge additions by the play they enable, not the mod count. [S1–S4]

Not decided yet

The server name, intended audience, player count, session schedule, permanent-world policy, difficulty, PvP, economy details, progression-sharing rules and reset policy are still open. A private friends-only co-op server is the recommended starting assumption, not a recorded final decision.

2. What a Minecraft server actually is

There are two useful meanings of “server” in this plan.

The technical server runs the shared world simulation. It manages things such as blocks, entities, inventories, game rules and saved progress. Each player's client provides their view of that world, takes input and communicates with the server. A smooth-looking client does not by itself prove that world simulation or network response is healthy.

The player-facing server is the whole experience around that simulation: the world, content, rules, access, community, onboarding, progression and support. A working modpack is one part of this, not the complete design.

Our current hosting direction

Confirmed provider direction: Space-Node Minecraft hosting, https://space-node.net/minecraft . Likely starting allocation: 12 GB RAM, subject to the final tier, region, price and workload checks. No purchase or deployment is recorded by this brief. This replaces local e4mc/LAN sharing as the intended production hosting route. [S10]

The shared world will run in a dedicated Minecraft server process on the provider’s infrastructure. “Dedicated server process” does not mean an exclusive physical machine or dedicated CPU cores. All players, including the owner, connect as clients. The owner’s PC will no longer need to stay on for the shared world to remain available, provided the hosted service is running. Actual uptime, maintenance windows and restart behaviour still need confirmation.

Price reference, not a quote: The retrieved provider FAQ advertises MC Special 1 with 12 GB at €10.80/month. The supplied URL’s text extraction returned general homepage/FAQ content rather than a complete Minecraft plan comparison, and a direct page request was blocked with HTTP 403. Treat this as an indicative advertised price only, not a verified price for the selected region/tier or a tax-inclusive checkout total. [S10]

Developer implications: Prepare and test a dedicated-server distribution for the selected Minecraft/NeoForge version. Audit mod side requirements rather than copying the client instance wholesale; keep client-only graphics, shaders and interface components off the server unless their server support and purpose are established. Players still need matching required gameplay mods. Verify custom-pack upload, the exact NeoForge/Java runtime, startup controls, file access, CPU allocation, storage and backup/restore facilities before committing to the plan. Generic Forge or modpack support does not prove this exact NeoForge pack works.

Region recommendation: Prefer Singapore if most players are in Singapore, subject to actual plan availability, pricing and measured network response. The provider lists Singapore among its regions, but the selected plan’s region, CPU tier and stock are not confirmed. Do not infer European advertised hardware or prices apply to Singapore. [S10]

Capacity and player experience: Treat 12 GB as a starting candidate, not proof of player capacity or smooth ticks. Test a busy starter town, machines, entities/combat and simultaneous exploration at the intended player count. The hosting RAM allowance is not automatically the Java heap size; leave room for non-heap/native use within the provider’s actual limit. Set heap and performance budgets only after checking the service configuration. Plan backups and off-host exports, scheduled maintenance, whitelist/access control and operator permissions before opening the server.

3. What players can build their experience around

The following systems are documented in the main development baseline. This is a content map, not certification of every recipe, feature or multiplayer interaction. [S2–S4]

Agreed core-loop and balancing direction

The owner accepted the recommendations in the priority-decision table. These are agreed planning directions, not invented numerical settings or finished implementations:

  • Core loop: Prepare → explore or run a dungeon → earn XP, currency and useful rewards → improve character/equipment → tackle a new goal. Building and industry remain valid parallel activities rather than mandatory adventure gates.
  • Progression pace: Map the first session, early game, midgame and endgame before assigning XP curves, prices or unlock pacing. No level caps, milestone rewards or durations have been selected.
  • Player death: Keep inventory and lose progress toward the next RPG level without losing earned levels or unlocked abilities. Normal respawn is at the bed; instance respawn is at the portal entrance. A full party wipe, not an individual party-member death, resets the instance. Major rewards are granted at completion; no currency penalty is selected.
  • Economy: Balance equipment-token conversion and automated farming together, with explicit earning routes and spending sinks. No automatic farm-income cap, sale price or tax is selected.
  • Encounter difficulty: Make danger understandable before commitment. The current owner-selected open-world rule is increasing mob level/rank with distance from the starter town/hub; details follow below.
  • Loot ownership: Explicitly govern personal versus shared rewards, tokens, boss drops and NPC kill attribution. While in a party, distribution follows party settings; the actual modes, defaults and eligibility still need design. XP sharing alone does not settle loot sharing.

Approved next-decision recommendations (items 2–7)

The owner approved the suggested starting points in the next-decisions table, items 2–7. Record the following as agreed direction, not completed configuration:

  • Starting character/progression: Explain starting race/class choices before commitment and allow early build mistakes to be corrected without restarting. Exact starting options, correction mechanism, eligibility window and any cost remain open.
  • Distance-based danger: Use readable danger bands rather than an invisible increase every block. Protect onboarding areas from high-level mobs brought in from farther away. Band boundaries, level/rank values and enforcement are unselected.
  • Party rewards: Show the active party loot rule before combat. Share leveling XP with nearby, eligible members rather than everyone merely listed on the roster. Exact distance, participation criteria, offline/dead handling and whether the rate bracket uses roster or eligible count remain open; the fixed 100%/55%/40%/40% schedule is unchanged.
  • Economy/farming: Establish normal adventure earnings versus farm earnings before setting plot and upgrade prices. Balance prices against a common economy baseline rather than independently. This approval does not introduce a specific farming cap, tax or conversion value.
  • Dungeon rules: Explain difficulty, rewards and failure rules at entry and define safe return before elaborate rewards. The confirmed death/reset/portal-entrance policy remains authoritative; survivor handling and other detailed run rules still need decisions.
  • Combat/protection: Start with cooperative defaults. Invincible mounts must not make riders immune or bypass encounter mechanics. Exact PvP consent/enablement, friendly fire, mounted attacks and damage-source controls still need specification; do not infer universal rider invulnerability or a fully selected PvP policy.

Approval is limited to those six table recommendations. The later acquisition/ownership suggestions and launch-scope discussion have not thereby become final decisions.

Approved acquisition direction

Basic mount travel should be accessible early. Mount variety and desirable collectibles should reward exploration and progression. Exact starter access, species, acquisition sources and pet/NPC recruitment remain open; no free mount kit or specific purchase is implied.

Player death, inventory and dungeon reset

Confirmed baseline: Keep inventory is on. Death deducts XP from progress toward the next RPG level, without removing earned levels or unlocked abilities. Normal deaths respawn at the player’s bed. The exact deduction remains to be tuned; define the zero-progress floor and calculation explicitly. No additional Minecraft enchantment-XP or currency penalty is selected. Retain supported equipment, bags and modded slots without duplicate death drops.

Confirmed instanced-dungeon exception: A dead player respawns at the instance’s portal entrance rather than their bed, retaining inventory and taking the defined RPG XP penalty. One party member dying does not reset the run while the party has survivors. A full party wipe resets the instance; a solo player dying is a wipe in a solo run. Bind the correct safe entrance outside the cleanup area before admission. This exception does not automatically apply to ordinary world dungeons.

Confirmed reset/reward direction, remaining lifecycle details: Reset occurs on a full party wipe. Major rewards are granted at successful dungeon completion, not incrementally from early encounters. Define the completion criterion and award each entitled recipient once, using the active party loot policy. Ordinary incidental loot/token and XP retention on failure remains open; major end rewards do not imply all earlier rewards are forfeited. Define wipe membership, including NPC companions, offline players, portal-respawned players and any re-entry/revive state so respawning immediately cannot make a wipe undetectable. Define whether reset regenerates the layout, re-entry during an ongoing run and safe handling of companions/pets/mounts before cleanup. Prevent repeat completion or wipe/reset races from duplicating rewards.

Fallback and recovery decisions: Define bed-missing/blocked behaviour, any respawn-anchor interaction, and recovery when a recorded portal is missing or its destination is unloaded. Select a safe fallback rather than leaving the player stranded. Persist death penalties and reset/return state so disconnect/restart cannot apply an XP deduction twice, skip it or repeat instance cleanup. Concurrent party deaths should produce one coherent reset, not conflicting teardown operations. Decide return/re-entry and companion cooldown handling explicitly.

Acceptance target: Exercise normal death with a valid bed, invalid/no bed and the agreed fallback. Verify inventory/equipment and supported modded slots persist without duplicate death loot; deduct exactly the configured XP once in the correct progression system. In an instance, verify correct portal-entrance respawn, safe handling of all occupants, a complete reset and the selected retained-reward/re-entry policy. Test simultaneous deaths, disconnect during respawn/reset, restart and missing return portals; ensure no persistent-world area is reset. Game rules, code and runtime behaviour have not been changed or verified by this documentation update.

Complete playstyle routes, not mandatory cross-mod progression

Confirmed design principle: The modpack must offer varied activities while allowing players to pursue their goals through magic alone, exploration alone or technology alone, without being forced to personally progress through the other disciplines. These are complete alternative playstyles, not partial roles that become dependent on a mixed-discipline party. Players may mix them voluntarily. This strengthens the earlier sandbox-choice requirement: the absence of explicit quest gates is insufficient if essential rewards still require another discipline in practice.

Shared outcomes, different methods: Each route needs a viable way to develop power, obtain useful equipment/resources, earn and spend universal currency, access the server’s shared services and sustain long-term goals. Comparable opportunity does not require identical mechanics, rewards or progression speed. Magic does not need to build an industrial machine to count as resource production, and a tech-focused player must not be forced into repeated dungeon combat merely to use shared upgrades or housing. Preserve the separate flavour of each discipline rather than making every mod interchangeable.

Scope boundary: “Achieve anything” is recorded as access to shared server outcomes and meaningful progression, not a promise that a player can operate a specific mod’s machinery without using that mod or acquire every discipline-specific ability without its native requirements. Which signature items/abilities remain exclusive versus which outcomes need alternate access must be made explicit. This boundary is a planning interpretation to confirm when defining those rewards, not permission to impose hidden mandatory cross-discipline gates. Existing travel, flight, claim and party rules still apply to every route.

Integration work to design: Map important goals to magic, exploration and tech acquisition routes. Identify missing sources of character XP, currency, upgrade materials, essential resources and shared unlocks for each route. Alternative recipes, magical production, exploration rewards, server exchange or trading are possible solutions, not implementations approved by this requirement. Optional player trade should enrich specialization rather than be the only escape from a hard dependency on an unavailable specialist. Do not assume unlimited shop stock or let one cheap conversion erase every other activity’s value. Reconcile major dungeon rewards with this principle: exclusive dungeon trophies can retain identity, but rewards required for shared progression need a viable non-dungeon path for dedicated magic/tech players.

Acceptance target: Demonstrate a representative early-to-late progression route for each dedicated playstyle, including a useful first result, ongoing earnings/resources and access to agreed shared goals without personally leveling another discipline or relying on another player to remove a mandatory blocker. Record any exception explicitly. Audit recipes, acquisition, XP sources and reward gates, not just mod presence; current mods have not yet been shown to satisfy this requirement. Select future additions for missing gameplay opportunities, not mod count, and prefer supported configuration or narrow integration over adding overlapping mods.

Industry and a useful base

Modern Industrialization supports the industrial direction. AE2, Storage Drawers and Sophisticated Backpacks provide different storage roles. A player could focus on a workshop, repeat production, organised materials and useful supplies for the group.

Experience to establish: Start with one useful product, expand production and connect it to a storage/crafting workflow that actually works. MI–AE2 power and transfer interfaces must be verified rather than assumed.

Magic and personal power

Ars Nouveau, Iron's Spells 'n Spellbooks, Occultism and Nature's Aura provide distinct magic directions. Tensura, SoloCraft: Reawakening and Tensura-Leveling add character-growth and related companion/power systems.

Experience to establish: A player can understand a starting route, reach a useful first ability and understand its costs and limits. Co-installation does not prove shared mana, levels, unlocks or universal cross-mod scaling. Exact awakening, evolution and bridge behaviour still need feature-specific evidence.

Exploration, encounters and loot

Twilight Forest, Bosses of Mass Destruction, Tensura-related encounters, SoloCraft gates, Dungeons and Taverns and YUNG's Better Dungeons provide adventure anchors. Artifacts, Relics and related integrations add equipment interests. Tectonic contributes terrain rather than an authored campaign by itself.

Experience to establish: Players can prepare, discover a destination, understand entry and danger, earn something useful and return. Death recovery and dimension exits are part of the experience, not support details to leave until later.

Unified economy, characters and mob progression

Confirmed requirements: One currency used across the whole server; one coherent stats, classes and races framework integrating Tensura and Solo Leveling; and levels plus ranks for all mobs. These apply across the hub, persistent world and instanced dungeons, not only one adventure route.

Currency: Players should not need to juggle unrelated balances for different server economic systems. Retained shops, services, player trading and monetary rewards must use or explicitly reconcile to the common currency rather than silently maintain competing spendable balances. Which of those systems exist, the currency name, item versus account representation, earning routes, prices, money sinks and treatment of existing emerald/gold/crystal trades remain open. Skill points, mana and crafting materials are not automatically money; classify each existing resource before converting it.

Character framework: Present stats, class and race as distinct parts of one understandable character profile. Map the actual Tensura and Solo Leveling mechanics into that framework, with an explicit authority for shared values and rules for conversion, stacking, unlocks and persistence. A unified screen alone is insufficient if combat and progression still use contradictory values underneath. Do not assume both mods provide equivalent race/class systems, rename a native mechanic without tracing it, or grant the same reward twice through two progression hooks. Exact stat names, formulas, class/race rosters, evolution/awakening interactions, resource-pool handling and respec rules remain undecided.

All mobs have levels and ranks: Cover vanilla and modded mobs in the persistent world and dungeon instances. The scope includes hostile, passive and neutral mobs, bosses, villagers and summoned/tamed companions where represented as mobs; behaviour and scaling policies for each category still need design. Do not silently narrow “all mobs” to enemies. Level is a numeric progression measure; rank is a categorical tier whose names, ordering and meaning must be defined. Whether either changes attributes, rewards or only communicates status remains open. Labels alone do not establish balanced combat.

Current danger direction: distance from the starter town/hub. Farther from the starting town/hub means higher mob levels and ranks, expressed through readable danger bands. Protect onboarding areas against high-level mobs transported inward. Treat this as the current open-world design, not automatic player-level scaling. Define the authoritative hub reference point, distance metric, bands/curve, level and rank bounds and assignment timing before implementation. Multiple destinations or player-placed Waystones must not silently become new low-danger origins. Clarify other dimensions, vertical distance, bosses and dungeon-instance difficulty separately; instanced dungeons do not yet have a selected distance analogue. Preserve the all-mob coverage requirement while designing category-specific effects so harmless residents are not automatically made hostile.

Danger persistence and communication: Decide whether a mob retains its spawn-assigned level/rank when moved nearer the hub or is recalculated, including reloads, transformations and summons. Prevent dragging or teleporting powerful mobs into onboarding areas from bypassing the town safety policy. Show the relevant danger before travel or entry where possible; labels and scaling effects need separate tests. Exact distances, formulas and party-size difficulty adjustments are not selected.

Player-facing outcome: Players can understand their character, compare an encounter’s level/rank and use the same currency wherever the server supports economic transactions. Onboarding must explain what those values mean without promising equal difficulty from equal labels. Neither unified character data nor mob tiers automatically creates mandatory cross-mod progression gates.

Developer decisions and safeguards: Audit the exact installed mods and existing bridge before choosing an integration approach. Define authoritative server-side values, reliable save/rejoin/restart behaviour, migration of existing profiles/balances, permission-controlled adjustments and transaction history. Test against duplicate/negative/overflowing balances, repeated rewards, conflicting stat modifiers and farm/summon reward loops. Decide how levels/ranks are assigned at spawn, retained on reload and affected by transformations or summons; avoid unintentionally scaling harmless town residents into combat threats. No code, config change or integration mod is selected by this brief.

Custom Tensura skills for unsupported mobs

Confirmed requirement: Create a new custom Tensura skill catalogue covering all mobs that do not already have support through Tensura-compatible mods or integrations. The coverage goal is to bring otherwise unsupported mobs into the server’s Tensura skill framework, not merely give them level/rank labels. No final skill list or working integration is supplied by this revision.

Coverage first: Inventory exact mob entity IDs and audit existing Tensura integrations per entity and feature. Classify each as already supported, partially supported, unsupported or unverified; a mod loading alongside Tensura is not proof of skill support. Include vanilla and modded mobs, passive/neutral mobs and bosses rather than silently limiting coverage to hostile encounters. Preserve verified existing mappings and fill genuine gaps without duplicate grants. Treat owned pets, party companions, mounts and summons explicitly under their existing safety/reward rules.

Catalogue to author: These are Tensura Custom Skills associated with mobs as absorbable skill data, not additional mob combat abilities. The mob is described as having the skills but does not activate their effects; the association is effectively cosmetic/inert until an eligible player absorbs a skill through the appropriate Tensura ability and owns it as a functional player skill. This acquisition route is owner-specified design intent; exact-version absorption support still needs verification. Record mob/entity ID, existing support/evidence, Custom Skill ID/name, thematic association, player effect, absorption eligibility and player-use costs/cooldowns where applicable. Exact roster, mapping cardinality, absorption conditions/chances and restrictions remain open. Do not substitute automatic skill awards on ordinary kills or add skill-driven mob AI.

Integration boundaries: Reconcile skills with unified character stats/classes/races and verify the selected Tensura version’s actual registration, assignment, acquisition and persistence hooks before selecting implementation. Custom skills must not restore independent player flight, bypass claims or override pet/mount invincibility and companion capacity rules. Any acquisition system needs explicit handling for automated kills, party attribution, summons and repeated respawns to prevent unintended reward farming; do not award the same skill twice through native and custom hooks.

Acceptance target: Reconcile the entity inventory against existing and new mappings so no in-scope mob is silently omitted. Validate skill IDs, effects and any approved acquisition separately, preserving existing supported behaviour. Test representative passive, hostile, boss and owned/summoned entities across world/dungeon contexts, save/rejoin, transformations and respawns; verify restrictions, duplicate prevention and combat workload. Actual catalogue authoring, compatibility audit, implementation and gameplay tests remain pending.

Equipment customisation and upgrades

Confirmed direction: Equipment and armour support anvil-based customisation through a special UI, paid only with the server’s universal in-game currency, not real-world money. One target piece is changed in an operation: it can adopt another item’s appearance or special ability, but cannot take that item’s stats. Equipment also supports enhancement, enchantment and socketing as separate progression systems. No implementation is selected or tested.

Two distinct operations: Appearance transmog changes the target’s look. Ability transfer changes its special ability using another item as the source. Ability transfer is functional customisation, not merely cosmetic transmog. Neither operation copies the donor’s numeric equipment stats. Whether appearance and ability changes can coexist on one target, whether an ability replaces or adds to an existing ability, and any item/category restrictions remain open. “One piece” is recorded as one target per operation, not a confirmed one-slot-per-character limit.

Proposed UI flow: Interact with an anvil, select one target and a source item, choose appearance or ability transfer, inspect the preview and displayed cost, then confirm. Charge only the universal in-game server currency, with no real-money payment or checkout for this feature; any additional material/XP requirements and the actual price remain undecided. The UI must distinguish what changes, what stays and what happens to the source item. Source consumption, reusable unlocks, removal/reversion and restrictions are not settled. This is a proposed interaction design, not proof vanilla anvil behaviour implements it.

Stats boundary: The operation must preserve the target’s numeric equipment stats rather than import the source item’s damage, armour or other stat values. A special ability may itself affect combat or temporary attributes; define eligibility and those effects explicitly rather than promising that ability transfer can never affect effective combat output. Retain existing enhancement, enchantment and socket data unless a separately approved rule says otherwise.

Separate upgrade tracks: Enhancement, enchantment and socketing are confirmed features, but their exact mechanics are open. Define enhancement effects/caps/costs and whether there is any failure or downgrade risk; define enchantment availability, compatibility and limits; define socket counts, eligible inserts, effects and removal rules. Do not assume upgrade levels, random failure, item destruction, gem types or stat bonuses. The no-stat-copy restriction applies to appearance/ability transfer; it does not establish what the separate upgrade tracks may modify.

Acceptance target: On representative vanilla and modded equipment/armour, verify appearance and ability operations separately, unchanged target stats, intended ability effects and preservation of existing upgrade data. Check previews against server-authoritative results, valid/invalid source pairs, insufficient funds, cancellation, repeated confirmation, concurrent access, disconnect/restart and save/rejoin. A failed operation must not lose items/currency or duplicate the output/ability. Prove enhancement, enchantment and socketing separately, then their approved combinations with customisation.

Equipment mob tokens and filtered redemption

Confirmed requirement: When a killed mob would drop equipment, replace that equipment drop with an equipment mob token that can be claimed later. During redemption, the player can set keep-filters. Matching results become equipment; results that fail the filters convert directly into the server’s universal in-game currency, without creating the rejected equipment item. The intent is to prevent farms flooding the server with random equipment, not to remove all mob loot or grant equipment for every kill.

Player flow: Kill a mob → receive token entitlement instead of its equipment drop → redeem later with chosen keep-filters → receive matching equipment or currency for rejected results. Exact redemption location/UI and token representation remain undecided. Non-equipment drops are outside this replacement rule unless separately approved. Coverage is intended for equipment drops from vanilla and modded mobs in both world exploration and dungeon runs; exact equipment eligibility and any exceptions need an explicit definition.

Deferred reward boundary: Resolve the candidate’s properties server-side sufficiently to evaluate filters and currency value, then create deliverable equipment only on the keep branch. Do not implement rejection as spawning/delivering equipment and immediately selling or deleting it. Whether loot is fixed at death or rolled at redemption is open; preserve applicable loot context and prevent disconnects, retries or preview/filter changes from granting extra rolls. A logical reward descriptor is not a dropped equipment entity; some mod APIs may still require temporary in-memory item data, so implementation must distinguish that cost from world entities, inventory delivery and full item generation. Exact supported hooks are unverified.

Filters and valuation still to design: Candidate filter fields could include equipment type, rarity/rank, stats or special abilities, but these are examples, not an approved schema. Define available fields, AND/OR behaviour, defaults, unavailable-property handling, confirmation and whether filters apply per claim or batch. Display when a rejected reward will be irreversibly converted. Set currency valuation and farm-economy limits explicitly; do not assume mob rank equals equipment rarity or silently introduce a second currency.

Performance safeguard, not measured improvement: Physical tokens can also create large numbers of item entities, especially if each carries unique data and cannot stack. Prefer stackable/aggregated tokens or another compact entitlement representation, subject to preserving reward context and ownership. Decide the representation before claiming the system solves farm lag. Bound redemption batch size and per-tick work so opening many tokens does not merely move the lag spike to redemption. Benchmark entity count, tick cost, retained reward data and redemption load against equivalent direct-drop farming; no lag reduction or capacity claim is established yet.

Ownership and failure decisions: Define token pickup, storage/trading, automated-kill attribution, expiry if any, group ownership, and claim limits. A full inventory must use a defined recovery policy, not silently lose kept equipment, auto-sell it outside the chosen filters or flood the ground with items. Token consumption, currency credit and kept-item delivery require recoverable, server-authoritative transactions. Repeated clicks, automation, concurrent redemptions, disconnects and restart must not duplicate rewards or consume a token without its outcome. Audit mob loot hooks so original equipment and replacement tokens cannot both be awarded.

Acceptance target: Test representative vanilla/modded equipment drops, multiple eligible drops from one death, non-equipment preservation, player and automated kills, and world/dungeon contexts. Verify configured filters against known candidate properties; rejected outcomes credit the correct universal currency and create no equipment entity or delivered equipment item. Kept outcomes match their candidate data and consume the correct entitlement once. Test full inventories, cancellation, large batches, concurrent claims, retry/disconnect and save/restart. Check token aggregation and stored context, then measure farm and redemption workloads separately. Implementation and gameplay tests remain pending.

Travel: Waystones and collectible shared mounts

Confirmed direction: The main modes of transport are Waystones and collectible mounts. The collection includes ground, water and flying mounts. Mounts are summonable and invincible, and must accommodate more than one player riding together. Disable anything that lets players fly independently of a mount. These are player-experience requirements, not evidence that a selected mod already supports this combination.

Two complementary travel routes: Use Waystones for destination-to-destination travel and summon an owned mount for journeys across the world with friends. Proposed mount flow: choose an unlocked mount, summon it at a valid location, board as driver, allow passengers to board, travel and dismount safely. Mount acquisition, collection UI, summon control and dismissal mechanism are unselected. Walking and ordinary swimming are not prohibited by naming the main transport systems; policies for other vehicles and teleport systems still need reconciliation.

Ground, water and flight: Ground mounts serve overland travel, water mounts serve water routes, and flying mounts provide the approved way to fly. Do not assume every mount works in every environment or that each has identical speed, handling or seating. Multi-player seating is a required capability, not an optional cosmetic passenger pose: the driver and at least one additional player must actually ride together. Exact capacities and whether capacity differs by mount remain open; do not silently select a single-seat-only roster.

No independent player flight: Audit vanilla equipment and all installed mod powers, spells, races/classes, transformations, accessories and machines for routes that grant personal flight, including elytra-style gliding, jetpacks and creative-style flight where available. Those are review targets, not claims that each is installed or has a supported disable switch. Disable flight functionality server-side using supported configuration or narrow overrides where possible, preserving unrelated progression and item uses. Do not merely hide recipes or rely on client controls: existing items, commands, unlocks and alternate grants also need coverage. Distinguish ordinary jumping, swimming and brief knockback from controllable flight; borderline mobility such as sustained levitation needs an explicit rule. Any administrative/build exception must be separately agreed, not assumed for survival players.

Invincible mount does not mean invincible riders: Mount damage must not kill the mount under the agreed invincibility contract; test combat, environmental hazards and modded damage/removal paths. Decide recovery from void, forced removal, invalid locations and dimension unloads separately from health immunity. Rider damage, drowning/breathing, falling, attacks from the saddle and dismount protection remain open. Do not automatically make players immune while mounted or let an invincible entity become an unreviewed combat shield. Mounts are not loot-farming targets: reconcile the all-mob level/rank requirement without granting death rewards from damage attempts, dismissal or resummoning.

Passenger permissions and lifecycle: Define who can board or steer: owner, invited players, group members or others. Integrate any group-based access with the unified player-group rules without granting passengers base or claim privileges. Set safe outcomes for owner/passenger disconnect, death, logout, dismissal, server restart, dimension changes, Waystone travel and a full/invalid landing area. Do not silently teleport passengers, strand them in the air/water or duplicate a collected mount. Collection ownership must persist independently of the currently summoned entity. Whether an owner can summon multiple mounts concurrently remains undecided.

Open travel policies: Mount acquisition and rarity, speeds, seat capacities, summon restrictions/cooldowns/costs if any, collection trading, dimension and dungeon access, indoor use and combat boarding/dismissal. Define Waystone discovery/unlocks, public versus private destinations, placement, permissions, cross-dimension use, travel fees if any and whether riders/passengers can travel together. Any monetary fees use universal in-game currency; no price or fee is committed. Invincibility does not override claims, town protection or dungeon access rules. Set speed and loading budgets against new-terrain generation on the chosen host rather than promising unrestricted high-speed exploration.

Acceptance target: Demonstrate collection unlock and persistence, summon/dismiss/re-summon without duplication, ground/water/flight handling and at least two actual players riding each offered mount under its approved seating rules. Test access denial and driver/passenger control separation, hazards and modded attacks, safe boarding/dismount, disconnect/rejoin, death, restart and permitted dimension/Waystone transitions. Verify survival players cannot obtain independent flight through each audited source, including pre-existing items and alternate grants, while approved mounted flight still works. Test Waystone unlock/permission and failure recovery separately. Measure multi-rider travel, active mount counts and fast new-terrain exploration on the target server. No implementation or runtime acceptance is recorded.

Pets: idle, following and respawning

Confirmed direction: Players can own pets. Idle pets are invincible. If an idle pet is moved, it teleports back to where it was idling after a period of time. A player may bring only one pet with them at a time. While following, that pet is immune to environmental damage but can be damaged by other mobs. If killed, it respawns after a timer. Both the idle-return delay and death-respawn timer are undecided. This does not establish a one-pet ownership limit.

Idle state: Record the pet’s idle location and dimension when the owner intentionally leaves it idle. Accidental displacement must not silently redefine that location. After the configured delay, return a displaced idle pet to its recorded idle spot. Deliberately moving that spot needs an explicit owner action, with its control still unselected. Idle invincibility is distinct from anchoring: movement may occur, but the timed return corrects it. Define safe handling when the spot is obstructed, removed or unavailable; do not teleport into solid blocks or force-load chunks indefinitely just to enforce the return.

Following state: One pet accompanies a player; other owned pets do not follow alongside it. The single-following-pet limit must hold across pet mods and concurrent commands, not separately per mod. Environmental immunity must not become blanket invulnerability: mob attacks must still damage a following pet. Define damage-source classification for fire, lava, drowning, falls, suffocation and modded hazards, including mixed sources such as mob-fired projectiles or mob-applied burning. Damage directly caused by players, friendly fire and PvP-related effects remains an open rule; permission to take mob damage does not settle player damage.

Death and respawn: A following pet killed in combat enters a respawn cooldown rather than permanent loss. Preserve its identity and ownership; record the cooldown and recreate only one live pet when recovery is permitted. Exact respawn destination, health, automatic versus owner-triggered return, online/offline timer behaviour and post-respawn idle/follow state remain undecided. Decide whether another pet may fill the following slot during cooldown, while ensuring the respawn cannot create a second follower. Preserve intended names/progression/equipment through the agreed recovery rules; do not assume native mod death handling already does so. Pet deaths/respawns must not duplicate equipment, currency, tokens or combat rewards.

System boundaries and open decisions: Define which vanilla/modded tames and summons count as pets; do not automatically classify an entire summoned army as pets. NPC party companions are the explicit separate category defined below: they use party slots rather than the one-following-pet limit. Pet rules and mount rules are distinct: a following pet is not an invincible mount. Decide any dual-role entity’s policy explicitly. Reconcile pet levels/ranks with the all-mob framework, group permissions and owner control without granting group members ownership. Acquisition, collection limits, behaviour/attacks, healing, switching controls and dungeon/dimension access remain open. One follower does not bound the number of idle entities; set idle-pet storage/simulation policy against actual server load without inventing an ownership cap.

Travel and lifecycle: Define how the selected following pet handles mounted travel, Waystones, portals and dungeon entry/cleanup; do not assume it occupies a passenger seat or keeps pace with a flying mount. Handle owner death, logout, disconnect, pet chunk unload and server restart explicitly. Persist idle anchors and respawn state, prevent a live entity plus respawn duplicate, and avoid timer resets that allow permanent loss or instant recovery. Returning idle pets must not bypass claim/town protections.

Acceptance target: Demonstrate idle invincibility, displacement and timed return to the original anchor, safe handling of blocked/unloaded destinations, and intentional anchor changes. Verify exactly one following pet across supported pet sources, environmental immunity, mob damage and the agreed player-damage policy. Exercise mob-caused death, timer expiry, single respawn, ownership and approved data preservation. Test switching pets during cooldown, repeated requests, owner death, disconnect/rejoin, restart, Waystone/dimension travel and dungeon cleanup. Confirm no duplicate pet or death reward and measure idle-pet/follower load. No pet implementation or runtime acceptance is recorded.

Four-member parties and shared leveling XP

Confirmed direction: Players can form parties with a maximum of four total members. Real players and NPC companions both occupy party slots. NPC companions follow the pet lifecycle/protection logic, but their accompanying limit is the four-member party cap instead of the one-following-pet limit. The controlling player counts as a member: a lone player can therefore party with up to three NPC companions. Examples are one player plus three companions, two players plus two companions, or four players; none may exceed four total members.

Confirmed leveling-XP schedule: Let B be the eligible leveling XP that the event would award to one solo player, before party distribution. Use the following percentage per member, not an equal division of a fixed 100% pool:

  • One member: 100% of B to that member; total awarded is 100% of B.
  • Two members: 55% of B each; total awarded is 110% of B.
  • Three members: 40% of B each; total awarded is 120% of B.
  • Four members: 40% of B each; total awarded is 160% of B.

The four-member rate deliberately remains 40% each; do not normalize it to 25% or silently reduce the bonus. For a base reward of 100 leveling XP, the respective per-member awards are 100, 55, 40 and 40 XP. These are mathematical examples of the specified rates, not selected mob rewards. The schedule creates a larger combined reward for a full party, while an individual receives less than solo.

NPC companion behaviour: Idle NPC companions are invincible and return to their idle anchor after a displacement delay. While accompanying their party, they are immune to environmental damage but vulnerable to mob damage; death triggers timed respawn rather than permanent loss. Reuse the pet system’s open damage-classification, anchor, recovery and timer decisions rather than invent a separate safety policy. NPC companions use party capacity, not the pet slot; whether a player may simultaneously bring a separate pet alongside a full party remains an explicit decision. Distinguish NPC companions from mounts, ordinary idle pets and any other summons when applying the cap.

XP interpretation and remaining authority decisions: The working design interpretation is that NPC companions count toward the party-size bracket and receive that bracket’s share into their own leveling progression, not an extra share paid to their owner. This follows the shared party logic but needs reconciliation with the actual companion progression systems; do not invent NPC levels or award redirected XP if unsupported. Define which unified character-leveling events supply B and where existing bonuses apply. Minecraft enchantment XP, skill-specific XP, money and loot are not automatically included. Define rounding/fractional carry so small awards are not silently lost or exploited. Prevent native XP plus bridge XP awarding the same event twice.

Party-configured loot ownership: When players are in a party, their party’s settings determine loot distribution. This is confirmed, not an assumption that every reward is automatically shared. Define the supported modes and default, who may change them, how members see/accept the active rule, and when a rule takes effect. Personal loot, shared loot, round-robin or rolling are possible designs, not approved modes. Explicitly cover equipment mob tokens, boss rewards and NPC/pet kill attribution; define how this policy reconciles with Lootr and other native personal-loot systems before promising uniform control. Non-party loot ownership remains to be decided. Snapshot the applicable rule and recipients per reward event so changing settings cannot duplicate or reassign an already-owned reward. Currency and skill acquisition are not automatically loot, and party loot settings must not silently change the confirmed XP schedule.

Membership is not claim access: Integrate party identity and invitations with the unified grouping design, but keep temporary party membership separate from permanent FTB team/guild membership and claim permissions. Partying with someone or their NPC must not automatically grant access to their land. Party XP sharing is now confirmed; shared inventory, currency, loot, quest completion and race/class unlocks are not implied.

Eligibility and lifecycle still open: Nearby eligible members, not the entire listed roster, receive party leveling XP. Decide the exact sharing distance, same-dimension requirements, participation/AFK rules, kill attribution for NPCs and pets, and handling of offline, dead or respawning members. Explicitly define whether the rate uses roster size or eligible recipient count; do not silently count an absent companion to increase the bracket or redistribute its share to the owner. Define invite/leave/kick/leadership, companion recruitment and removal, respawning slot reservation, disconnect/rejoin and dungeon admission/re-entry. Freeze an authoritative eligible roster per reward event so changing membership cannot duplicate or reallocate an already-issued award. Party capacity must hold across interfaces, concurrent invitations and companion respawns.

Acceptance target: For controlled base rewards, verify the four exact per-member rates and aggregate awards with all-player and supported mixed player/NPC parties. Reject a fifth member, including simultaneous invitations or respawn races. Verify each recipient is paid once into the intended leveling system, rounding is consistent, unsupported NPC progression is not fabricated, and owner XP is not multiplied by companion shares. Exercise range/dimension, AFK, death/cooldown, disconnect/rejoin, party changes and restart against the selected rules. Prove NPC idle return, environmental immunity, mob vulnerability and timed respawn separately. Confirm temporary parties do not change permanent claim access. No party implementation or gameplay test is recorded.

Two dungeon routes

Confirmed direction: Players can choose hub-accessed, instanced procedurally generated dungeons or head into the shared world to discover normal mod-added dungeons. These are parallel adventure options, not a requirement to complete one before accessing the other.

Hub route: Prepare at the hub, enter a separately managed dungeon run, explore its procedurally generated layout and return through a defined exit/recovery flow. “Instanced” means the run is logically separated from the persistent exploration world; whether instances belong to individuals, parties or another grouping remains to be designed. The hub’s location within or alongside the starter town is also open.

World route: Travel through the persistent world and its applicable modded dimensions, discover dungeon structures, tackle their encounters and return with loot. These locations follow the selected mods’ world-generation and persistence rules; they are not automatically reset or isolated per party. Some world dungeons may also generate procedurally: the distinction is the run lifecycle and access route, not simply random versus fixed layouts.

Player experience to preserve: Players understand which route they are choosing, its entry conditions, risks, reward rules and how to get home. Neither route should silently become a mandatory prerequisite for the other. Exact reward balance remains open; this does not promise identical loot or difficulty.

Developer decisions still needed: Compatible instancing/generation implementation; solo/party access and ownership; entry costs and cooldowns, if any; difficulty and rewards; join-in-progress and re-entry; death/item recovery; disconnect and server-restart recovery; reset/expiry and safe cleanup; and limits on simultaneously active or generating instances. Do not assume existing mod gates provide true instancing or select a new mod solely from this requirement.

Hosting implication: Test dungeon creation, active runs and cleanup alongside the persistent world’s base/town workload. The tentative Space-Node 12 GB plan has not been validated for this combined load. Instancing does not imply a separate paid server per run or unlimited parallel runs.

5. Proposed player journey

The starter town and player onboarding are confirmed requirements. The detailed journey below is proposed for developer planning. Its milestones are outcomes to design and test, not instructions or features that already exist in full.

Before joining

Give players one approved pack revision, clear Prism import steps, the hosted-server address/access and maintenance arrangement and a short rules page. Explain required gameplay content separately from optional graphics. Check the weakest player's hardware before setting a shared performance expectation.

Success looks like: A new player can install the right revision and understand when and how to join without a long support conversation. Join/rejoin works without losing progress.

Starter town and first-session onboarding

Required outcome: New players begin their server experience through a starter town with onboarding. Developers should treat the town and onboarding as one connected player journey, not a decorative spawn build with instructions added later. Exact spawn placement and return behaviour remain open implementation decisions.

Proposed onboarding flow:

  1. Introduce the fantasy/tech RPG SMP premise and the purpose of the starter town.
  2. Explain the essential rules, multiplayer expectations and where to get help.
  3. Explain four-member parties, NPC party slots and the leveling-XP schedule. Explain pet idle/follow modes, the one-following-pet limit and timed respawn. Explain Waystone travel, collecting/summoning mounts, passenger boarding and the no-independent-flight rule. Show the few everyday tools players need first: recipe search, block identification, inventory controls and navigation.
  4. Explain mob-associated Tensura Custom Skills and their absorption into functional player-owned skills once exact eligibility is defined. Explain the unified player-group system, claims and access rules, as well as the unified currency, character stats/class/race and mob level/rank meanings. The actual values and rules must match the implemented system.
  5. Explain equipment mob tokens, later redemption and keep-filters versus automatic currency conversion, then introduce the available starting directions: technology, magic, character growth, adventure and home-building. Explain the choice between hub dungeon runs and world exploration, without requiring immediate dungeon entry.
  6. Explain keep-inventory, the configured XP death penalty, bed respawn and instance portal-return/full-party-wipe reset rules. Explain increasing danger with distance from the starter town/hub and how to inspect party loot settings before an expedition. Show the optional purchasable town/hub plots and the alternative of open-world chunk claiming. Players may use either or both, and do not have to buy a plot.
  7. Give the player a clear next step out of onboarding and into their chosen activity.

Developer decisions still needed: Town layout and theme; how instructions are delivered; spawn/respawn and return access; protection and build permissions; whether any starter supplies are provided; how returning players revisit help or skip material they already know. NPCs, shops, quests, starter kits and forced tutorial completion are not approved merely by requiring a town.

Acceptance target: A player unfamiliar with the pack can navigate the town, find essential help, understand the rules, choose a starting direction and identify their next action without a developer guiding every step. Onboarding must not require understanding the entire pack or revealing late-game spoilers. The selected starter route should then lead to a useful first result. This is an untested target, not a completed feature.

First few sessions

Offer short starter routes for industry, magic, character progression, adventure and home-building. Explain individual prerequisites and any genuine cross-system dependency. Keep deeper spoilers optional.

Success looks like: The player can name their next personal goal and one thing they can do with friends. They can return to their base and continue after saving and rejoining.

Ongoing play

Let personal projects and shared expeditions run alongside each other. Possible group goals include a workshop, shared supply area, settlement or planned boss trip. These are examples, not approved custom quests. A unified server economy is now required, but particular shops, prices and project rewards remain undecided.

Success looks like: Progress makes new activities or useful outputs possible. A player who joins later or plays less often can still contribute. Whether this needs catch-up rules must be discussed rather than silently changing mod progression.

6. Player-experience principles to adopt

These are recommendations for the next design discussion, not settings already applied.

  • Choice without confusion: Show a small set of understandable starting routes, then let players choose.
  • Cooperation without forced dependence: Make shared projects worthwhile without automatically requiring every player to complete every mod.
  • Challenge with a reason: Check existing powers against existing encounters before adding harder bosses or changing rewards. Current imbalance is a risk to investigate, not an established defect.
  • Recoverable setbacks: Explain death, lost items, dimensions, disconnects and hosted-server restarts. Decide the recovery policy before disputes happen.
  • Comfort before spectacle: Optional visuals must not become a barrier to joining. Smooth play, readable interfaces and usable controls matter more than screenshots.
  • Useful help, optional spoilers: The wiki should answer “what can I do next?” and “how do I do this?” with deeper mechanics clearly separated. A comprehensive wiki is the goal, not a completion claim.

7. Decisions that materially change the server

Answer these in roughly this order. They are the missing product decisions, not reasons to restart pack selection.

  1. Who is it for? Friends-only or a wider community; expected concurrent players; weakest client; host capacity.
  2. When can people play? Space-Node removes the owner-online dependency. Confirm the intended availability, scheduled group sessions, maintenance/restart windows and what players may progress while others are absent.
  3. How is the confirmed identity expressed? The server is a fantasy/tech RPG SMP. Decide the relative emphasis on adventure/power, industry and settlement-building, without reopening that identity as an unresolved genre choice.
  4. How does life beyond the starter town work? The onboarding town is confirmed. Decide whether players then establish separate bases, expand a common settlement or use another arrangement within the required unified grouping system; define storage, claims, theft, destructive-spell and PvP rules. The starter town does not imply everyone must live there.
  5. What counts as fair progression? Personal versus shared rewards, absent-player progress, boss access, companion ownership and help for late joiners. Existing bridge shop/shared-combat features should not be enabled just to fill this gap. [S6]
  6. How is the confirmed death policy completed? Set the next-level RPG XP-progress deduction, valid-bed/portal fallback, wipe membership detection, retained incidental rewards and re-entry. Keep inventory, no lost earned levels/abilities, bed versus portal respawn, full-wipe reset and major completion rewards are confirmed.
  7. Which world becomes permanent? Final terrain/structure selection, exploration boundaries and reset/update expectations. Upcoming worldgen must pass disposable-world checks first.
  8. How do dungeon runs work? Both adventure routes are intended. Set instance ownership, access, difficulty/rewards, run lifecycle, failure/re-entry rules and concurrency limits; establish where the dungeon hub sits relative to the starter town.
  9. How are economy and progression unified? Define the currency and supported transactions, authoritative character fields, Tensura/Solo mappings, class/race rules and mob level/rank assignment/scaling. This is required design work, not a claim that the current bridge solves it.
  10. How does equipment customisation work? Set donor consumption, compatible item pairs, appearance/ability coexistence, ability replacement/stacking, prices and reversibility. Define enhancement, enchantment and socketing independently and reconcile them with unified stats.
  11. How are groups unified? Verify native real-player guild support in the selected Tensura/Solo releases, map any supported features to FTB Teams/Chunks and choose membership authority, roles, claim permissions and lifecycle rules. Keep temporary expedition access distinct from permanent base access.
  12. How do equipment mob tokens work? Define equipment eligibility, token aggregation/ownership, fixed-at-death versus redemption rolls, filter fields/defaults, currency valuation and bounded claim processing. Protect against duplicated loot, lost outcomes and farm-driven inflation.
  13. How does shared travel work? Choose mount acquisition and roster, multi-player seat capacities, summon and rider safety rules, group-based passenger access, Waystone unlocks/permissions and dimension/dungeon restrictions. Audit and disable independent flight without stripping unrelated character features.
  14. How do pets behave? Define eligible pets versus summons/mounts, idle-return and respawn timers, safe return/respawn destinations, damage-source rules, switching during cooldown and travel/restart handling. Keep ownership limits distinct from the confirmed one-following-pet limit.
  15. How is party XP awarded? The four-member cap and rates are fixed. Define base-XP sources, bonuses, NPC progression recipients, range/participation, dead/offline eligibility, roster versus eligible-count brackets and rounding; reconcile NPC respawn slots and the separate pet allowance.
  16. Which Tensura Custom Skills fill coverage gaps? Audit existing per-entity mappings and author absorbable mob skill associations. Mobs do not activate these skills; player-owned effects become functional after eligible Tensura absorption. Define exact skill roster, absorption rules and player balance.
  17. How do optional town plots work? Set plot sizes/prices, ownership and co-builder rules, protection boundaries, supply/limits and transfer/abandonment policy. Preserve independent open-world claim access.
  18. How is distance-based danger expressed? Define hub origin, distance metric, bands/formulas, rank/level bounds, moved-mob handling, other dimensions and dungeon exceptions. Define party loot modes/defaults, setting-change authority and integration with native loot systems.
  19. How does each dedicated playstyle reach shared goals? Map magic, exploration and tech sources of power/XP, currency, materials and shared unlocks. Resolve mandatory cross-discipline dependencies and distinguish signature content from shared outcomes.
  20. What does the server deliberately not offer? No Create is established. Public access and authored quests are not commitments. A unified currency, unified character systems and mob levels/ranks are confirmed requirements; their implementation details remain open. Space-Node hosting is the selected direction, but a specific uptime guarantee is not established.
